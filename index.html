The problem

Knowledge is exploding

Every call, every chat thread, every document, every AI conversation is knowledge — and it accumulates faster than any person can keep up with, let alone write down properly. Most of it is never captured, and what is captured scatters across a dozen places nobody will search later.

The volume compounds. Each week adds more calls, more threads and more documents than the week before, while the hours available to organize any of it stay the same.

How it works · Personal knowledge base

Organizing each team member’s knowledge work

basezero connects to the tools people already use — email, Slack, Claude, and call-notetaker tools like Jamie — and organizes what they produce into one knowledge base a person can open and read, rather than a folder they have to search.

The knowledge base leads its own maintenance. When two names turn out to refer to the same project, it surfaces the conflict, drafts the correction and asks the person to confirm which one is right.

How it works · Company enablement

Dissolving silos into a compounding knowledge edge

What one person learns does not have to stay with that person. Individual knowledge is translated into organizational knowledge without compromising what is sensitive — so every colleague’s work adds to the same structure instead of ending in a private note.

Nothing crosses from personal to organizational without a person approving it. The inbox and the share center put their proposals in front of someone — with the audience and the reason attached — and anything sensitive is held back at the same gate.

01

Share and revoke, one platform

Share knowledge with team members and with agents from one place, and revoke it the same way. Revoke a source and every answer derived from it loses access too.

02

Sensitivity, always kept

Permissions live on the fact rather than the file, so one conversation can be shared in part. What has to stay closed is held back at the same gate.

03

Agent-to-agent-to-human workflows

Agents work the open questions between themselves to close knowledge gaps across teams, then hand what needs a decision to a person. Silos break without anyone stopping to document.

04

A knowledge feed, not an intake pile

Overwhelming intake becomes a routine: what changed, what needs confirming, and what is worth sharing, in the order it matters.

A person approves every change that crosses from individual to organizational. This is a human-in-the-loop workflow, not an automated sync. basezero drafts, proposes and explains; someone decides, on the record.

Fact-level sharing

Technical capabilities

Knowledge in one system, owned by you

Most AI tools search a folder. This is a knowledge graph — it ingests your organization’s knowledge, resolves it into a single structure, reasons across it, and keeps proving it’s right. Five layers, working together.

01Ingestion

One graph, kept current, automatically

Every source — AFAS, Exact, DATEV, Personio, SharePoint, Drive, Slack, your file shares — syncs on a schedule, not a one-time import. As content lands, it’s resolved into entities and typed relationships, so “your biggest customer” is one node no matter how many systems mention them. The graph tracks the entity, not the file. No manual uploads. No stale context six weeks after go-live. No migration project every time a source changes.

Incremental syncChange detectionDeletion propagationPermission refreshEntity resolution
02Retrieval

Finds the whole answer, and says so

Most assistants fire a single query and have no way to know what they missed — there’s nothing in their architecture to enumerate against. This layer knows the edges of its own corpus. It walks all of it, follows a question across as many hops as the answer requires, and reports what it covered, not just what it found. Ask the same question about last year and it retrieves what was true then, not only what’s true now. Every claim traces back to the passage it came from.

03Transparency

See the answer. See the evidence. See the source.

Every answer arrives with its ranked sources and a direct line to the underlying documents — not a black box you have to take on faith. What the graph concludes on top of your raw data is written to a living wiki: one page per customer, product or process, every claim linked back to where it came from. Anyone can open it, search it, correct it in place. No embeddings you can’t inspect, no query language to learn, nothing hidden.

04Curation

Gets better because people use it

The graph watches itself — surfacing gaps, contradictions and pages going stale, with the missing piece already drafted for a person to approve. It also learns from how your team actually communicates, reasoning across the graph instead of only retrieving what’s already written down. Most AI tools write what they learn into a private memory only one person can read. This one gets smarter for whoever asks next.
